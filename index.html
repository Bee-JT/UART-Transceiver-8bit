Engineering Adaptation Record • Platform Migration

Hardware Migration from AMD Xilinx Artix-7 to Gowin GW1NR-9C (Tang Nano 9K)

The transceiver logic was originally specified, coded, and simulated for the departmental AMD Xilinx Artix-7 (XC7A100TCSG324-1) evaluation board referencing a 50.0 MHz system clock. Following an institutional laboratory equipment policy constraint where Artix-7 hardware access was restricted to senior capstone tracks, the team executed an immediate architecture migration to maintain physical bring-up progress.

The platform was migrated to the team-procured Sipeed Tang Nano 9K board, driven by the Gowin LittleBee GW1NR-9C (GW1NR-LV9QN88PC6/I5) FPGA. This change required refactoring the clock prescaler modulus from 50 MHz to the onboard 27.0 MHz oscillator, transitioning the compilation toolchain to Gowin EDA V1.9.9, and authoring native physical pin constraints (tang_nano_9k.cst).

Current Sprint Objective

Isolated Transmitter (TX) Physical Layer Silicon Validation

In accordance with sound hardware verification practice, rather than attempting simultaneous loopback testing on uncharacterised silicon, the active sprint concentrates solely on the physical bring-up of the Transmitter (TX) module.

The transmitter state machine continuously or strobedly emits structured ASCII character frames (e.g. repeated 0x55 or text strings) through Pin 17 directly into the Tang Nano's onboard USB-to-UART bridge. Telemetry is decoded on a host terminal emulator (PuTTY / Tera Term) at 9600 Baud (8-N-1) to verify edge transitions, stop-bit holding times, and framing stability before advancing to receiver (RX) oversampling integration.

Chronological Audit Trail

Laboratory Engineering Notebook

Official engineering log entries recording architectural decisions, hardware roadblocks, and verification status.

ENTRY 01 • Baseline Architecture • Gowin EDA Formulation

Microarchitecture Specification & Native Gowin EDA Simulation

Formulated the initial UART microarchitecture specifications. Configured the project environment natively within the Gowin EDA (Gowin YunYuan) suite. Authored behavioral Verilog RTL modules and executed testbench simulations using Gowin EDA's integrated simulation tools, validating finite state machine transitions, 8-N-1 packet framing compliance, and clock division accuracy at 9600 Baud.

ENTRY 02 • Roadblock • Hardware Reallocation

Departmental Board Access Policy Constraint & Alternative Sourcing

Encountered a critical roadblock during hardware scheduling: the ECE department announced that Artix-7 FPGA development kits were reserved exclusively for final-year capstone batches. To avert schedule delays, the team evaluated viable low-cost FPGA alternatives and procured a Sipeed Tang Nano 9K development board equipped with the Gowin GW1NR-9C FPGA, seamlessly matching our Gowin EDA project environment.

ENTRY 03 • GowinSynthesis • Clock Recalibration

Prescaler Math Porting to 27.0 MHz & Gowin Physical Pin Constraints

Synthesised the design using GowinSynthesis in Gowin EDA V1.9.9. The prescaler modulo counter was mathematically recomputed for the Tang Nano's 27.0 MHz crystal oscillator:
DIVISOR = round(27,000,000 / 9600) = 2813 • Synthesized: 9598.29 Baud • Error: -0.018%
Authored physical pin constraints (tang_nano_9k.cst) assigning the master clock to Pin 52, tactile reset to Pin 4, and serial transmission out to Pin 17.

ENTRY 04 [ACTIVE SPRINT] • Transmitter Validation

Gowin Bitstream Generation & Standalone TX Bring-Up

Currently executing standalone physical verification of the Transmitter logic. Bitstreams generated via Gowin EDA's Place & Route flow (.fs file) are loaded into GW1NR-9C SRAM via the Gowin Programmer. The hardware emits repeated ASCII character streams through Pin 17 directly across the onboard USB-to-UART bridge into PuTTY at 9600-8N1. Confirming clear frame delineation, stable 104.2 μs bit times, and zero buffer overruns before implementing the 16× oversampling RX module.
